Research

Chronic Condition Networks

NIDI  ·  Prof. Solveig A. Cunningham  ·  2024 to present

I use network approach on SHARE panel data in R to examine how multimorbidity clusters and how it changes over time across 16 chronic conditions. Cardiovascular and metabolic diseases consistently form the core of these networks and act as strong predictors of newly acquired conditions. Currently I'm working on the accumulative effects of chronic conditions.

The Co-evolution of Social Networks and Environmental Opinions

Utrecht University  ·  Prof. Vincent Buskens  ·  2024 to present

I build an agent based model in Python to study how fixed individual characteristics (education, for example) interact with network tie formation to produce or prevent consensus on environmental attitudes. The model generates predictions about social cohesion under different network structures using multilevel models.

Sexual Orientation, Leisure, and Health Disparities

Master's thesis  ·  Dr. Deni Mazrekaj  ·  2025 to present

My thesis asks whether health gaps between sexual minority and heterosexual individuals are explained by differences in leisure participation. I use the LISS panel and estimate parallel mediation models with Coarsened Exact Matching to reduce selection bias. Cultural activities turn out to explain the better physical health of sexual minorities compared with heterosexuals.

Immigrant Career Trajectories in Germany

Utrecht University  ·  Prof. Ineke Maas  ·  2024 to present

Working with 40 waves of the GSOEP (1984 to 2023), I estimate Latent Growth Curve Models to compare how immigrants and natives move through occupational careers over time. Immigrants tend to reach a lower peak status, but matching shows they arrive at equivalent status levels, just later.

Trust Networks Across Social Groups

Fudan University  ·  Prof. Felicia F. Tian & Prof. Xue Li  ·  2023 to 2025

Constructed interpersonal trust networks for 31 subgroups from six waves of the World Values Survey. I computed betweenness and closeness centrality across groups defined by income, education, and age to characterize how trust structures vary. Manuscript under review.

Conference Presentations

Modeling the Longitudinal Clustering Dynamics of Chronic Condition Networks

Wang Z, Banzato E, Cunningham SA  ·  Dutch Demography Day, Utrecht, 2026  ·  37th REVES, Tokyo, 2025  ·  Travel Grant

Applied Dynamic Bayesian Networks to SHARE panel data to examine how multimorbidity structures change over time across 16 chronic conditions. Cardiovascular diseases and arthritis emerged as predictors of later conditions.

The Social Networks of Chronic Diseases

Wang Z, Cunningham SA  ·  36th REVES, Tampere, 2025  ·  ODISSEI, Utrecht, 2025

Used Social Network Analysis on SHARE Wave 9 to map co-occurrence patterns of 16 chronic diseases. Identified three disease clusters and found cardiovascular conditions, arthritis, cataracts, and Alzheimer's disease to be the most central nodes in the network.

Absolute or Relative Educational Attainment: Education and Intergenerational Social Mobility

Wang Z, Liu X  ·  Chinese Sociological Association Annual Meeting, Harbin, 2024

Using China General Social Survey data (2008 to 2021), applied Bootstrap Mediation Analysis to compare how relative and absolute educational attainment shape intergenerational status transmission as education expanded over time.

Socioeconomic Status, Lifestyle, and Health Inequality

Wang Z, Tian FF  ·  Chinese Sociological Association Annual Meeting, Tianjin, 2023  ·  Wangdao Scholar Award

Estimated Latent Basis Growth Models on nine waves of the China Health and Nutrition Survey. Compared cumulative health trajectories across white-collar, blue-collar, and service workers, finding that early lifestyle behaviors play a decisive role in long-term health accumulation.

Community Integration of Cross-Border Marriage Immigrants

Wang Z  ·  Chinese Sociological Association Annual Meeting, Tianjin, 2023  ·  Outstanding Project Award

Based on four months of fieldwork in Yunnan Province using Respondent-Driven Sampling, interviewed 15 Burmese women married to Chinese men. Identified two structural dimensions and four distinct patterns of community integration.
